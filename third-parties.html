Are you a third party?

Many advocacy organisations cross a threshold without planning to. Advertising or material about an election issue can count as electoral or political expenditure, and once spending passes a threshold, registration and disclosure obligations follow.

TierJurisdictionWhen it appliesMain obligations
Third partyFederal (AEC)Electoral expenditure above the disclosure threshold in a financial yearAnnual return
Significant third partyFederal (AEC)Electoral expenditure above $250,000 in the financial year or any of the previous three, or other criteriaRegistration, annual return, donation disclosure
Third-party campaignerVictoria (VEC)Political donations received or political expenditure above $5,030Registration, disclosure, donation caps

Not sure where you sit? A short conversation is usually enough to work it out. A self-check is coming to the Knowledge Hub.

What we do

  • Expenditure tracked by candidate and electorate, so spending is measured against the cap that applies to each.
  • A consistent approach to political expenditure, recorded in one policy so similar spending is treated the same way each time, with borderline calls referred to a qualified adviser.
  • Registration, returns and disclosures on one calendar, across federal and state jurisdictions.
  • Donor disclosure at volume, with donation platforms reconciled to Xero so small-dollar donations are ready to report within the required window.
  • Fractional CFO oversight: budgets, cashflow forecasting, and board and funder reporting.
  • Readiness for 1 January 2027, when the federal disclosure and expenditure rules change.
